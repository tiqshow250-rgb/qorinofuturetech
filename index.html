02 — Frontier One · Quantum Technology

Quantum: the picks-and-shovels layer.

Quantum computing exploits superposition and entanglement to perform certain calculations exponentially faster than classical machines. But the most urgent unmet need is not another qubit modality — it is the infrastructure layer: cryogenics, control electronics, error-correction software, and application-specific sensors.

◆ Quantum Technology

The Technology Behind It

A quantum computer is not a single machine but a tightly coupled ecosystem: a cryogenic dilution refrigerator, a quantum processor unit (QPU) with signal delivery, room-temperature control electronics, and classical compute for error decoding and compilation.

Leading modalities include superconducting qubits, trapped ions, photonic qubits, and silicon spin qubits. The industry is converging on modular architectures where multiple QPUs are linked via quantum interconnects to scale beyond the wiring bottleneck of monolithic chips.

Near-term revenue is concentrated in quantum sensing — gravimeters, magnetometers, and atomic clocks for navigation and defence — which do not require fault-tolerant computing.

Key Machinery & Equipment

ComponentFunction

Error-correction decoderReal-time syndrome decoding

Major Challenges (2026)

  • Wiring bottleneck. Each superconducting qubit typically requires an individual signal line. A fault-tolerant machine may need a million or more physical qubits — linear scaling is untenable.
  • Error rates. Practical computation requires qubit error rates below 10⁻¹⁰; current hardware is orders of magnitude away.
  • Materials and fabrication uniformity. Qubit coherence is limited by materials defects and device-to-device variability.
  • Cryogenic electronics gap. Cryo-CMOS and single-flux-quantum logic remain immature.
  • Photonic loss. Fusion-based photonic computing is limited by probabilistic photon sources and massive multiplexing needs.
  • Standardization. The field lacks agreed metrics for "quantum advantage," muddying enterprise procurement.

Solutions Being Pursued

  • Flip-chip integration of qubits and cryogenic control electronics into a single multi-chip module breaks linear control-line scaling.
  • Digital demultiplexing distributes control pulses to multiple qubits, reducing wire count.
  • Thermal-noise-resilient microwave quantum networks at 4 K decouple qubit temperature from channel temperature.
  • Silicon spin qubits exploit established semiconductor manufacturing for density and uniformity.
  • Quantum sensing delivers near-term revenue without fault tolerance.
Qorino FutureTech Play Anchor venture: Quantum Sensing-as-a-Service for defence and infrastructure — quantum gravimetry for subterranean mapping and quantum magnetometry for submarine detection. Derivative venture: a quantum-classical hybrid software platform that benchmarks and integrates quantum optimization into enterprise workflows, monetizing the gap between hype and adoption.
03 — Frontier Two · Semiconductors & Chips

Semiconductors: application-specific silicon.

The AI compute boom has exposed fragility in the semiconductor supply chain. Energy efficiency, advanced packaging, and edge inference are the new battlegrounds. A startup cannot outspend TSMC or NVIDIA on fabrication — it can win by designing application-specific architectures for the other frontiers Qorino operates in.

◆ Semiconductors & Chips

The Technology Behind It

Modern semiconductors are manufactured through deposition, lithography, etch, implantation, and packaging. The critical enabler of advanced logic is extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography, which uses 13.5 nm light generated by firing a high-power laser at tin droplets.

The industry is transitioning from 0.33 NA EUV (N2, in production in 2026) to High-NA EUV (0.55 NA) for the angstrom era (A14 and beyond), and eventually to Hyper-NA (0.75 NA) around the A3 node (2039).

At the device level, the roadmap moves from FinFET to Gate-All-Around (GAA) nanosheets, then to Complementary FET (CFET) stacked architectures, and eventually to 2D channel materials at the A2 node (2041).

Key Machinery & Equipment

EquipmentFunction

Major Challenges (2026)

  • High-NA EUV transfer yield. Moving High-NA patterns into device layers with high yield requires innovations in resists, masks, deposition, and etch.
  • Mask field size reduction. High-NA halves the exposure field, forcing chiplet architectures and multi-die stitching.
  • Interconnect resistivity. Ruthenium and alternative metals are needed, but direct metal etch at aggressive pitches is difficult.
  • Memory wall. AI workloads demand greater density and bandwidth; 3D integration and new charge-trap materials are required.
  • Supply chain concentration. Advanced lithography and deposition are concentrated among a handful of vendors.
  • Cost escalation. A single High-NA EUV tool costs ~$400 million.

Solutions Being Pursued

  • Dry resist platforms have achieved single-exposure patterning for sub-20 nm ruthenium interconnects with 100% electrical yield.
  • Chiplet architectures and CoWoS packaging bypass reticle limits by stitching multiple dies.
  • Backside power delivery is introduced at A14 to reduce IR drop and improve performance.
  • 3D integration (wafer-to-wafer bonding) enables heterogeneous logic, memory, and photonics.
  • RISC-V and open-source EDA lower the barrier for application-specific edge AI silicon.
Qorino FutureTech Play Anchor venture: a fabless edge-AI inference chip company designing silicon for autonomous aerial systems and robotic platforms, using RISC-V and chiplet architectures to stay capital-efficient. Derivative venture: an advanced packaging design house helping deep tech startups move from prototype to production using chiplet-based architectures and domestic packaging partners.
04 — Frontier Three · EV & Drones

EV & Drones: aerial logistics take off.

The centre of gravity in electric mobility has shifted from passenger vehicles to aerial logistics and defence UAS. Battery energy density, autonomy stacks, and airspace integration are the critical bottlenecks — and the entry points.

◆ EV & Drones

The Technology Behind It

eVTOL aircraft and cargo drones are battery-electric or hybrid-electric platforms using distributed electric propulsion (DEP) for vertical lift and forward flight.

Core subsystems are high-energy-density batteries, high-voltage power distribution (800V platforms are emerging), electric motors, flight control computers, and detect-and-avoid (DAA) autonomy stacks.

For ground EVs the technology is more mature, but the convergence with drones lies in shared battery management, charging infrastructure, and energy-aware routing for collaborative delivery fleets.

Key Machinery & Equipment

ComponentFunction
High-voltage battery pack800V platforms reduce current and weight
Lithium-polymer cells79.4% of drone battery shipments in 2025
Electric propulsion unitMotor + inverter + propeller
Flight control computerAutonomy, stability, DAA
Battery swapping system9-minute fast charge for 80 kg-class drones

Major Challenges (2026)

  • Battery energy density vs. weight. Urban air mobility needs 400–500 Wh/kg or higher; current eVTOL cells exceed 300 Wh/kg.
  • Thermal management at high discharge rates. Take-off, hover, and landing require sustained high power in a lightweight package.
  • Standardization gap. Aviation-grade battery standards for wide temperature ranges and safety are not yet fully established.
  • Energy-aware routing. Jointly optimizing route planning, EV charging, and drone battery swapping is computationally challenging.
  • Airspace integration. BVLOS operations and urban air traffic management remain regulatory and technical bottlenecks.

Solutions Being Pursued

  • 800V high-voltage platforms have been flight-demonstrated in heavy-lift eVTOL, solving insulation, thermal, and lightweighting.
  • Solid-state and hydrogen fuel cells are being explored for longer-range missions.
  • Battery swapping and fast-charging ecosystems reduce downtime and improve unit economics.
  • Hybrid-electric architectures combine batteries with a small generator for extended range.
Qorino FutureTech Play Anchor venture: an autonomous cargo drone platform for middle-mile logistics and infrastructure inspection, with a modular payload system and a proprietary detect-and-avoid stack. Target paid pilots with logistics operators and utility companies within 18 months. Derivative venture: a battery pack and thermal management startup focused on high-cycle-life cells for drone and eVTOL applications.
05 — Frontier Four · Robotics & Physical AI

Robotics: dependable generality, not demos.

Robotics funding has surged past $27B annually, but the sector has entered a "show-me" phase. Winners solve specific, high-value bottlenecks in unstructured environments — not general-purpose humanoids chasing a consumer fantasy.

◆ Robotics & Physical AI

The Technology Behind It

Physical AI refers to embodied intelligence: robots that perceive, reason, and act in unstructured physical environments. The stack comprises high-performance actuators, multi-modal sensors (cameras, LiDAR, mmWave radar, tactile, torque, IMUs), edge compute, and vision-language-action (VLA) models trained via reinforcement and imitation learning.

Humanoid robots are the most visible embodiment, but the harder problem is general-purpose manipulation in dynamic, human-centric spaces. The field is moving from task-specific demos to dependable generality.

Key Machinery & Equipment

ComponentFunction
Smart actuatorHigh torque density, backdrivable joints
Torque sensorForce feedback for safe interaction
Tactile skinContact sensing for grasping
3D vision / LiDARScene understanding and avoidance
Edge AI computeOnboard VLA model inference
Precision reducerHigh-torque, low-backlash transmission

Major Challenges (2026)

  • Actuator performance envelope. Humanoids need actuators that are high-force, backdrivable, compact, efficient, and intrinsically safe — all at once.
  • Sim-to-real gap. Learned controllers often fail on physical hardware due to unmodeled dynamics and contact complexity.
  • Generality across tasks. Most learned policies remain task-specific and struggle with novel objects.
  • Data scarcity. Physical AI models require large-scale interaction data; frontier models are often closed-source.
  • Reliability and uptime. Many deployed robots operate fewer than 10 days per month — "shipping is not deployment."

Solutions Being Pursued

  • Backdrivable, high-torque-density actuators with series-elastic or rolling-contact transmissions narrow the dexterity gap.
  • Large-scale motion tracking datasets (e.g., SONIC, 700+ hours) improve generalization.
  • Zero-shot skill transfer methods (e.g., ZEST) allow policies to transfer across robots without retraining.
  • Inference-time adaptation frameworks compose new objectives without retraining.
  • Sensor fusion across cameras, mmWave radar, tactile sensors, and encoders improves robustness.
Qorino FutureTech Play Anchor venture: a mobile manipulation robot for hazardous industrial inspection (oil & gas, nuclear decommissioning, offshore wind), using VLA models fine-tuned for constrained environments with a clear ROI case based on reducing human exposure and downtime. Derivative venture: a robot fleet orchestration software layer allowing heterogeneous robots to operate in shared industrial spaces — the "Android of industrial robotics."
06 — Frontier Five · Rockets & Space Technology

Space: build the critical supplier.

The space economy is on a trajectory to $1.8T by 2035, but launch remains the toll booth. Rather than building a full launch vehicle, Qorino builds the critical supplier — with a clear path to strategic acquisition by a launch prime.

◆ Rockets & Space Tech

The Technology Behind It

Access to space is governed by the rocket equation, and cost is dominated by propulsion and propellant management. The industry is transitioning from expendable launchers to reusable systems, where the first stage (and increasingly the upper stage) is recovered, refurbished, and reflown.

Reusability demands variable-thrust, deep-throttling engines, health monitoring systems, and cryogenic propellant management that survives repeated thermal cycling.

Propellant choices are shifting: LOX/LCH₄ (methane) is favored for reusability due to lower coking and easier handling, while LOX/LH₂ remains the highest specific-impulse option for upper stages.

Key Machinery & Equipment

ComponentFunction
Variable-thrust liquid engineDeep throttling for landing and reuse
TurbopumpHigh-pressure propellant delivery
Cryogenic tankPropellant storage at ~90 K
Zero-boil-off systemEliminates propellant loss
Engine health monitoringReal-time diagnostics for reuse
Additive manufacturingComplex engine parts, fewer joints

Major Challenges (2026)

  • Cryogenic propellant management is the system-level bottleneck. Boil-off, stratification, chilldown, cavitation, leakage, sloshing, and tank durability dominate countdown risk.
  • Deep-throttling engine dynamics. Variable-thrust engines introduce severe nonlinearities; validated system-level models are lacking.
  • Reusability inspection and turnaround. Reusable systems must survive repeated cryogenic loading and thermal cycling without hidden damage.
  • Cost of propulsion. Liquid propulsion can be half of total launcher cost, making engine affordability the key to competitive prices.
  • Health monitoring for reuse. Reusability requires systems that predict remaining life and detect incipient failures.

Solutions Being Pursued

  • Zero-boil-off systems, digital twins, distributed sensing, and composite tanks improve propellant management.
  • Green high-thrust engines based on liquid hydrogen with additive manufacturing reduce part count and cost.
  • Edge AI and machine learning enable predictive maintenance for reusable systems.
  • Staged catalytic decomposition H₂O₂/kerosene engines offer a green, variable-thrust alternative.
  • Integrated system-level design combining thermofluid physics, monitoring, and operability is essential for 2026–2035 progress.
Qorino FutureTech Play Anchor venture: a reusable upper-stage technology company focused on a critical subsystem — a low-cost, restartable cryogenic engine or an advanced thermal protection system — sold to medium-lift launch startups with a clear path to strategic acquisition. Derivative venture: a space situational awareness (SSA) data platform combining ground-based radar, optical tracking, and onboard edge processing for collision-avoidance-as-a-service.

11 — Risk Management

Capital project discipline.

Deep tech is not a hackathon. It is a capital project with critical paths, milestone billing, and subcontractor management. Qorino runs it like one.

Critical
Technology Risk

Stage-gated capital. Each venture must hit technical milestones before the next tranche. No venture receives more than 30% of studio capital.

High
Founder Risk

An interim operator bench is maintained at the studio. Founder vesting is 4 years with a 1-year cliff. Board control provisions apply at early stage.

High
Capital Risk

Tranche 1 is sized to reach first spin-outs without Tranche 2. Shared services and government / defence contracts provide non-dilutive funding.

Managed
Regulatory Risk

A dedicated legal and regulatory lead from Day 1. All ventures undergo ITAR / EAR screening before formation. Jurisdictions selected for favourable regimes.

Managed
Market Timing Risk

The portfolio is deliberately uncorrelated across five frontiers. If quantum funding cools, space and robotics may accelerate. Cohort 2 re-weights toward strongest signals.

Managed
Concentration Risk

Each venture raises its own pre-seed / seed with Qorino contributing 20–40% and syndicating the rest to angels, pre-seed funds, and corporate VCs.

Structural
Fund-Cycle Risk

Evergreen structure avoids a 10-year clock. Qorino holds through the "valley of death" and captures value at inflection points instead of forced exits.

Structural
Talent Risk

Cross-frontier bench means engineers move between ventures as programmes peak and trough — retaining institutional knowledge inside the studio.
